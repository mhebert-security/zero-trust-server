about

I used to work somewhere the medication room was locked twice.

Five years of clinical nursing. The two locks were the design: people forget, people rush, and a single unlocked minute in that room has a price nobody should pay. So the system did not trust the person, it trusted the second lock. I carried that habit into software, where the same sentence is true and the price is your data.

That is why I write Rust, pointed at OT and ICS, the places where a bug and a person can meet. The code stays small, readable, and allergic to dependencies, for the same reason that room was locked twice. A codebase you can hold in your head is one you can reason about under stress. A dependency you cannot read is a promise you cannot check.

I am comfortable saying what I know and what I do not. I know what I have built, and you can read all of it, which is a better claim than any credential. The parts of this field larger than a single codebase are still ahead of me. I would rather say that plainly than perform certainty.

Based in Toronto. I write about the route from that room to here as it happens, unpolished and honestly labeled, on the writing page.
