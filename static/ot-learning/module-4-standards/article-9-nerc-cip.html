NERC CIP for Security Practitioners: What You Actually Need to Know

Published: 2026-10-02 · Module: Module 4, Article 9: Standards and Compliance · Code: github.com/mhebert-security/ics-standards-lab

I read IEC 62443 before I read NERC CIP. That order matters. IEC 62443 invites you to reason about your environment. NERC CIP does not invite you to do anything. It tells you what you must do, when you must do it, and what it costs you when you don't.

NERC CIP is the mandatory cybersecurity standard for the North American Bulk Electric System. Regional entities audit it. FERC enforces it in the U.S. with penalties up to $1 million per day per violation. Those numbers are real. They have been assessed.

Who It Applies To

The first question I had reading CIP was "does this apply to me?" That is the wrong way to approach the scoping exercise. The right question is "is this entity registered?"

Registration with NERC is the gate. If you are a transmission owner, a balancing authority, a generation operator, a reliability coordinator, CIP applies to you. The assets follow from the registration, not the other way around. I assumed scope was an asset question. It is a registration question, and the difference affects how you start the work.

The standards run from CIP-002 through CIP-014 and are versioned. The version in force in your region matters. NERC amends on a rolling basis. You cannot assume the version on a training slide matches what your regional entity is auditing against.

CIP-002 is the foundation. Every other standard asks "for your High and Medium Impact systems, what have you done?" If you miscategorize at CIP-002, the rest of your compliance program is built on the wrong floor. Auditors look at categorization before they look at anything else.

The Vocabulary

CIP uses defined terms precisely. Using them loosely causes compliance gaps. I got confused early because I was conflating terms that the standard keeps separate.

A BES Cyber Asset is a programmable electronic device that, if compromised or unavailable for 15 minutes, could affect the reliable operation of the Bulk Electric System. A BES Cyber System is a grouping of BES Cyber Assets performing a reliability function. You categorize systems. Not individual assets.

Electronic Access Control or Monitoring Systems are the systems that protect or monitor electronic access to BES Cyber Systems: firewalls, authentication servers, jump hosts, security information and event management platforms. They carry their own CIP requirements. Compromising an EACMS is often the prerequisite to compromising the BES Cyber System behind it, which is why the standard does not treat them as afterthoughts.

Protected Cyber Assets are systems within the Electronic Security Perimeter that are not BES Cyber Assets but share network space with them. They matter because they are lateral movement paths. An asset that is not itself safety-critical can still be the stepping stone to one that is.

BES Cyber System Information is information about BES Cyber Systems: design documents, network diagrams, security procedures, configuration files. CIP-011 governs how you store, access, and dispose of it. I remember reading this and thinking about a printed network diagram left on a conference table. That is a BCSI finding.

Transient Cyber Assets and Removable Media cover the laptops, USB drives, tablets, and vendor-supplied equipment that come into a physical security perimeter or connect to BES Cyber Systems. CIP-010 has specific requirements here. A vendor plugging a laptop into your control network without a documented authorization and a malware scan is one of the most common audit findings. It is also one of the easiest to prevent if the process exists.

What Evidence-Based Compliance Actually Looks Like

I used to think compliance was about having controls. It is not. It is about having evidence that controls ran.

CIP-007 requires patching within 35 days of release for High and Medium Impact systems, or a documented risk-based decision to delay. The patch is not the evidence. The ticket is: when you received the vendor notification, how you assessed the risk, when you scheduled the work, when you applied it, how you verified it. If that ticket does not exist, the patch did not happen from a compliance standpoint. The auditor does not know what you did. They know what you can show them.

CIP-004 requires personnel risk assessments before granting unescorted physical access or electronic access to BES Cyber Systems. The access cannot be granted before the PRA is complete. The access must be revoked within 24 hours of termination. Each of these events has a timestamp requirement. Manual processes drift. I could see, reading this, exactly how an organization ends up with terminated employees who still have active accounts three weeks later.

CIP-010 requires a baseline configuration for each BES Cyber System and documented authorization for every change. An engineer who remotes in and adjusts a parameter without a change ticket is a violation. The change may be operationally correct. It is still a violation.

Remote Access and the ESP

CIP-005 stopped me for an afternoon.

The requirement is that all interactive remote access to BES Cyber Systems has to originate from outside the ESP through an Intermediate System. The Intermediate System is the jump host. You cannot have a vendor VPN terminate directly at a BES Cyber Asset. The traffic terminates at the jump host, authenticates there, gets logged there, and the session gets monitored. Both the jump host and the path to the BES Cyber System behind it need to be covered.

MFA is required for all interactive remote access to High and Medium Impact systems. One-factor authentication to a jump host that then steps into a BES Cyber Asset without a second factor is not compliant. Both hops need coverage.

Vendor remote access is where I kept finding the gaps when I walked through fictional scenarios. The process has to be documented end to end: access requested, access authorized, session opened with logging active, session terminated when work is complete, access revoked. A persistent vendor VPN tunnel that stays up between visits is an always-open conduit that no one is monitoring. That is a finding.

Supply Chain: CIP-013

CIP-013 was added in 2020 and is still underestimated. It requires a documented supply chain risk management plan covering how you evaluate vendors, what security requirements go into contracts, how vendors notify you of incidents affecting your systems, and how you handle end-of-life software and firmware.

The mistake I kept seeing in walkthroughs was treating CIP-013 as a one-time procurement exercise. It is not. If a vendor notifies you of a software vulnerability in a product deployed on your BES Cyber Systems, you have obligations under both CIP-007 and CIP-013 at that moment. The response has to be documented. The connection between the two standards has to be thought through in advance, not after the notification arrives.

Contracts with vendors who have remote access or supply software to BES Cyber Systems need incident notification clauses, background check requirements, and the right to audit. Legal reviews these slowly. Start early.

What I Have Not Solved Yet

I have not mapped NERC CIP requirements to IEC 62443 Foundational Requirements systematically. The two standards overlap in the electric utility sector but are structured differently. CIP is prescriptive and auditable. IEC 62443 is a framework. Understanding where they align and where they create redundant work is the question a practitioner operating in both regimes needs to answer, and it gets its own article.

I have not worked through the violation and penalty process in depth. How violations are self-reported, how they are adjudicated, how mitigation plans work: that is a separate topic. The E-ISAC and NERC's public enforcement database are the primary sources for seeing how this actually plays out.
